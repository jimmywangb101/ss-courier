Google Maps

About 10 minutes Holding everything up

This is what measures the distance between the pickup and the delivery address, which is how the price gets worked out. Until it's live, the assistant can answer the phone and take all the details, but it can't quote — it hands the caller to a human instead.

This will be a completely new account of your own. Nothing is carried over from my side — the whole thing sits under your company from the very start, on your card, with you as the owner.

Create the account and project

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and sign in with the company Google account. If you've not used it before it'll offer you a free trial — accept it.
  2. At the top of the page there's a project dropdown, probably saying “Select a project”. Click it, then New Project.
  3. Call it something like Courier Booking System, click Create, then make sure it's the one selected in that dropdown.

Put a card on file

  1. Type Billing into the search bar at the top and open it.
  2. Click Link a billing account, then Create billing account.
  3. Enter the company details and card.

You'll almost certainly never be charged for this. Google gives a free monthly allowance that comfortably covers a business your size. But they refuse every single request unless a card is on file — that's the bit currently stopping us.

Turn the mapping on

  1. Search for Distance Matrix API.
  2. Open it and click Enable.

Worth being fussy here. It has to be Distance Matrix API specifically. Google has several similarly-named mapping services and the others won't work — this is the single most common thing to get wrong.

Set a spending cap

  1. Search for Budgets & alerts, then Create budget.
  2. Set it to £30 a month and tick the alert boxes (50%, 90%, 100%).

You'll now hear about anything unusual long before it matters.

Add me as a user

  1. Search for IAM and open it.
  2. Click Grant access.
  3. Under “New principals” put mohammadrabby.dil@gmail.com, give it the Editor role, and save.
Then just tell me Message me “Google's done” — I can see the rest from my end, and I'll confirm within a few minutes that pricing is working.

Twilio

About 20 minutes Approval takes 1–3 working days

This gives you the phone number itself, and sends the confirmation texts your customers receive.

Start this one early even though it's not blocking. UK phone numbers are regulated, so Twilio has to verify your business address before it'll issue one, and that check can take a few days. Everything else here is instant. Get this into the queue and carry on with the rest while it's being checked.

Create the account and upgrade it

  1. Sign up at twilio.com/try-twilio with the company email, and verify your email and mobile.
  2. Find Upgrade — usually top-right or under billing — and add the company card.
  3. It'll ask for an opening top-up. £20 is plenty to start with.

The upgrade isn't optional, I'm afraid. Free Twilio accounts can only text numbers you've verified one at a time, so real customers would simply never get their confirmation.

Submit your address proof

  1. Search the console for Regulatory Compliance and open Bundles.
  2. Click Create new Bundle, choose United Kingdom and that you're a business.
  3. Upload the utility bill or bank statement, fill in the company details, and submit.

That's the slow part done. Nothing else depends on it, so keep going.

Buy the number (once approved)

  1. Go to Phone Numbers → Buy a number.
  2. Set the country to United Kingdom.
  3. Tick both Voice and SMS in the capability filters, then pick a number you like.

It does need to be a UK number. There's a US one on the system at the moment, and UK mobile networks filter texts from US numbers heavily — they look like they've sent, but a lot of them never arrive.

Set a cap, and add me

  1. Search for Usage triggers and set an alert around £40 a month.
  2. Go to Account → Manage users → Invite user and add mohammadrabby.dil@gmail.com as an Administrator.

If your plan doesn't offer user invites, don't worry — there's a safe way to send me what I need further down.

Then just tell me Send me the new UK number once you've got it.

Cal.com

About 5 minutes

This is the calendar confirmed jobs land in.

  1. Sign up at cal.com with the company email. The free plan is fine to begin with.
  2. Go to Event Types → New. Title it Courier Collection, set it to 60 minutes, and save.
  3. Open Availability and set it to match when you actually operate. If you run around the clock, set it to all day, every day.

That availability matters more than it looks — the assistant checks it before it promises a slot to anyone.

Two things to send me

  1. The event type ID. Open the Courier Collection event you just made and look at the web address. It ends in a number, like .../event-types/123456 — that number is all I need.
  2. An API key, from Settings → Developer → API keys → Add. This one's a password, so please send it the safe way described below rather than by email.

Your booking records

About 10 minutes

The spreadsheet

  1. Create a new blank sheet at sheets.google.com and name the file something like Courier Bookings.
  2. At the bottom left, rename the tab from Sheet1 to exactly Bookings — capital B, since the system looks for that precise name.
  3. Send me the web address.

I'll send you back a long robot-looking email address ending in .iam.gserviceaccount.com. Hit Share on the spreadsheet, paste it in and give it Editor. That's what lets bookings write themselves into the sheet as they come in.

Confirmation emails

Customers get an email as well as a text, so the system needs permission to send from one of your addresses. I'd suggest making a dedicated one like bookings@yourcompany.co.uk rather than using your main inbox — that way it's isolated if it ever needs revoking.

  1. Sign in to that account and turn on 2-Step Verification at myaccount.google.com/security.
  2. Go to myaccount.google.com/apppasswords and create one called Courier Booking System.
  3. Google shows you 16 characters. Send those over the safe way below.

An app password sounds alarming but isn't. It's a single-purpose password that only lets software send mail — it can't be used to sign in and read your email, and you can cancel it in one click from that same page.

Hosting

About 10 minutes

At the moment everything runs on my computer, which means your line is only up when my machine is. Hosting moves it onto a proper server so it answers at three in the morning without me being awake.

  • Simplest: open an account at railway.app or render.com on the company card, around £5–10 a month, then invite me.
  • Or: if you already have a web host or an IT provider, just tell me who and I'll deal with them directly.

Once the account exists, the deployment is my end.

Sending me passwords safely

A couple of things above — the Cal.com key, the email app password — are effectively passwords. Please don't send those by email, WhatsApp or text. Those messages sit on servers and in backups more or less forever.

The easy way: go to send.bitwarden.com, paste the text, set it to expire after one day and one view, and send me the link it gives you. It's free and doesn't need an account.

If that's a faff, just ring me and read it out. Anything that isn't secret — the phone number, the spreadsheet link, the event type ID — is fine to send however you like.
